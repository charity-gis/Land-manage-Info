1. Executive Summary 
This document defines the product requirements for a digital Land Parcel Information and Transaction System 
(the
 
"System").
 
The
 
System
 
will
 
serve
 
as
 
the
 
authoritative
 
digital
 
record
 
for
 
land
 
parcels
 
—
 
starting
 
with
 
a
 
single
 
city
 
as
 
a
 
pilot
 
—
 
capturing
 
ownership,
 
location,
 
dispute
 
status,
 
and
 
sale/lease
 
status
 
for
 
every
 
parcel,
 
and
 
will
 
later
 
expand
 
to
 
cover
 
an
 
entire
 
country.
 
Unlike a passive registry, the System actively facilitates real-estate transactions by connecting verified 
sellers/lessors
 
with
 
prospective
 
buyers/lessees.
 
Because
 
fraudulent
 
land
 
transactions,
 
forged
 
titles,
 
and
 
boundary
 
disputes
 
are
 
widespread
 
risks
 
in
 
most
 
land
 
markets,
 
the
 
System's
 
defining
 
principle
 
is
 
that
 
no
 
parcel
 
record
 
is
 
published
 
or
 
updated
 
without
 
passing
 
through
 
a
 
mandatory
 
verification
 
workflow
 
performed
 
by
 
the
 
platform's
 
own
 
trained
 
Verification
 
Officers,
 
in
 
partnership
 
with
 
the
 
relevant
 
government
 
land
 
registry/survey
 
authority.
 
The System is architected as a multi-tenant, GIS-enabled platform that can scale from one municipality to 
hundreds
 
of
 
administrative
 
regions,
 
with
 
strong
 
identity
 
assurance,
 
immutable
 
audit
 
trails,
 
encrypted
 
document
 
handling,
 
and
 
role-based
 
access
 
control
 
at
 
its
 
core.
 
Guiding Principle 
Trust is the product. Every feature decision should be evalua ted against one ques tion: does this mak e it harder to publish false, fraudulen t, or unverified land informa tion?

2. Background & Problem Statement 
Land administration in many cities and countries is fragmented across paper registries, multiple disconnected 
government
 
offices,
 
and
 
informal
 
record-keeping.
 
This
 
creates
 
several
 
persistent
 
problems
 
that
 
the
 
System
 
is
 
designed
 
to
 
solve:
 
•
 
Buyers
 
cannot
 
easily
 
verify
 
true
 
ownership
 
before
 
purchasing,
 
leading
 
to
 
fraud
 
(e.g.,
 
sale
 
of
 
already-sold,
 
disputed,
 
or
 
government
 
land).
 
•
 
Sellers/owners
 
have
 
no
 
simple,
 
trusted
 
channel
 
to
 
reach
 
genuine
 
buyers
 
or
 
lessees.
 
•
 
Land
 
disputes
 
and
 
boundary
 
conflicts
 
are
 
not
 
visible
 
at
 
the
 
point
 
of
 
transaction,
 
so
 
buyers
 
acquire
 
liabilities
 
unknowingly.
 
•
 
Physical
 
land
 
records
 
are
 
vulnerable
 
to
 
loss,
 
damage,
 
tampering,
 
and
 
manual
 
forgery.
 
•
 
There
 
is
 
no
 
single,
 
searchable,
 
map-based
 
view
 
of
 
parcel
 
status
 
(owned/disputed/for-sale/leased)
 
for
 
a
 
city
 
or
 
region.
 
•
 
Manual
 
verification
 
processes
 
at
 
government
 
registries
 
are
 
slow,
 
inconsistent,
 
and
 
difficult
 
to
 
audit.
 
The System addresses these gaps by digitizing parcel records under a strict verification-before-publish model, 
and
 
by
 
building
 
a
 
marketplace
 
layer
 
on
 
top
 
of
 
verified
 
data
 
so
 
that
 
transactions
 
happen
 
with
 
confidence.

(see

5).
 
Relationship to the Statutory Land Registry 
In jurisdictions with an official governmen t land registry or national land informa tion system (e.g., a cadas tre or an e-registry such as Kenya's Ardhisasa/NLIMS), that governmen t system remains the ultima te legal sour ce of title. This System is designed to interoper ate with it: mirr oring and cross-checking verified data, acceler ating due diligence, and adding the mark etplace and independen t verific ation layer the governmen t system does not provide — not to replace statutory registration. Wher e no such governmen t system exists or is not yet integrated, the System's internal verific ation workflo w is the interim sour ce of trusted status until an official record can be cross-check ed.
